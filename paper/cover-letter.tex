\noindent To the Editors of \emph{Nonlinearity}

\bigskip

\noindent Re: ``The frozen blowup profile of the Okamoto, Sakajo and Wunsch
family: a control variate for its spectral exponent''

\bigskip

\noindent Dear Editors,

I am submitting the above manuscript for consideration as a research paper.

The Okamoto, Sakajo and Wunsch family was introduced in this journal
(\emph{Nonlinearity} \textbf{21} (2008) 2447), and the paper concerns the
frozen blowup profile it admits on the circle above a critical advection. That
profile's Fourier spectrum decays algebraically with an exponent $\beta$ which
has previously been obtained by least squares fits to the spectrum. I show that
fitting is accurate only to about a percent and is biased, and give a different
determination: the exponent follows from a single local quantity, and the
difficulty of reading that quantity off a discrete profile is removed by using
a second constant, known exactly, as a control variate. This yields five
digits, excludes the value $\beta = 3$ at $a = 0.8$, and corrects the published
exponent at $a = 0.71$ by $0.33$ percent.

I should draw your attention to two things. First, the setting is not mine: the
frozen profile was found numerically by Lushnikov, Silantyev and Siegel, and its
existence, stability and local exponent near $a = 1$ were proved by Chen. The
introduction and a Limitations section state in detail what is theirs and what
is mine, and the contribution is deliberately narrow. Second, the paper corrects
a value published by Lushnikov, Silantyev and Siegel. I have written to them
describing the discrepancy and offering the manuscript, before submitting here.

Every number in the paper is reproducible. The solver, the profile solver, the
scripts behind each table and a suite of nineteen checks against closed forms
and conserved quantities are openly available under an MIT licence at
\texttt{github.com/munawarkazmi/degregorio-blowup}, and the checks run on every
commit. I mention the address here rather than in the manuscript because the
repository identifies me and the manuscript is anonymised.

I request double-anonymous review. The manuscript is not under consideration
elsewhere, has not been published previously, and there are no competing
interests. I am an independent researcher with no institutional affiliation and
no funding to declare.
